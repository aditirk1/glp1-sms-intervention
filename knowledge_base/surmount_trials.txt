SURMOUNT-1 TRIAL - TIRZEPATIDE (GIP/GLP-1 DUAL AGONIST) - NON-DIABETIC ADULTS
Source: Jastreboff AM et al. N Engl J Med. 2022;387:205-216 (NCT04184622)
Population: Adults with obesity or overweight, no type 2 diabetes
Mean baseline BMI 38. 40% had prediabetes. Mean baseline HbA1c 5.6%.
Duration: 72 weeks. Doses: 5mg, 10mg, 15mg once weekly subcutaneous.

PRIMARY ENDPOINTS (efficacy estimand)
Tirzepatide 5mg: mean -16.0% body weight
Tirzepatide 10mg: mean -21.4% body weight
Tirzepatide 15mg: mean -22.5% body weight
Placebo: mean -2.4% body weight

CONFIRMED SECONDARY ENDPOINTS AT WEEK 72
HbA1c: mean 0.5% decrease across tirzepatide groups despite non-diabetic baseline
More than 95% of participants with prediabetes reverted to normoglycemia
Triglycerides: decreased more than 27% in pooled tirzepatide vs 6.3% placebo
Waist circumference at 72 weeks (15mg): approximately -19 cm
Source: PMC12381612 post-hoc SURMOUNT-1 and SURMOUNT-3 analysis
Body composition: fat mass reduced 33.9% vs lean mass reduced 10.9%
(approximately 3x greater fat reduction than lean mass reduction)
Blood pressure, lipids, waist circumference: all significantly improved vs placebo

METABOLIC IMPROVEMENTS INDEPENDENT OF SCALE WEIGHT
Source: Annals of Internal Medicine post-hoc SURMOUNT analysis
doi: 10.7326/annals-24-02623
HOMA-IR (insulin resistance) and HbA1c begin improving even with modest weight loss.
The steepest metabolic improvement occurs between 5% and 20% weight reduction.
This means insulin sensitivity is improving before the scale fully reflects
the medication's effects. A plateau on the scale does not mean insulin
sensitivity has stopped improving.

SURMOUNT-4 TRIAL - TIRZEPATIDE WITHDRAWAL AND MAINTENANCE
Source: Aronne LJ et al. JAMA. 2024;331(1):38-48 (NCT04660643)
Design: 36-week open-label lead-in (all received tirzepatide 10mg or 15mg),
then randomized 1:1 to continue tirzepatide or switch to placebo for 52 weeks.
670 participants completed lead-in. Mean age 48, 71% women.

LEAD-IN RESULTS (36 weeks, all patients)
Mean weight reduction: -20.9%

RANDOMIZED PERIOD (week 36 to week 88)
Continued tirzepatide: additional -5.5% weight reduction
Switched to placebo: +14.0% weight gain
Total at week 88: -25.3% (continued) vs -9.9% (placebo)
89.5% of continued tirzepatide patients maintained at least 80% of lead-in loss
Only 16.6% of placebo patients maintained at least 80% of lead-in loss

CARDIOMETABOLIC REVERSAL ON STOPPING
Source: Horn DB et al. JAMA Intern Med. Published November 24, 2025
Most patients who stopped tirzepatide regained more than 25% of body weight
within one year. This degree of regain was associated with significant reversal
of cardiometabolic improvements including waist circumference, non-HDL
cholesterol, and fasting insulin. Patients who maintained weight reduction
below 25% regain largely preserved their cardiometabolic gains.

KEY CLINICAL INSIGHT FOR PLATEAUED PATIENTS
A scale plateau during active treatment is entirely different from what happens
after stopping the drug. Patients who stay on therapy through a plateau preserve
their metabolic gains. Patients who stop lose them, and most regain the majority
of their weight within a year. Staying on through discouragement is not just
psychologically important. It is clinically protective.
