STEP 1 TRIAL - SEMAGLUTIDE 2.4mg ONCE WEEKLY - NON-DIABETIC ADULTS WITH OBESITY
Source: Wilding JPH et al. N Engl J Med. 2021;384:989-1002 (NCT03548935)
Population: 1,961 adults, no type 2 diabetes, mean BMI 37.9, mean age 46, 74% women
Duration: 68 weeks

PRIMARY ENDPOINT
Mean body weight change: -14.9% (semaglutide) vs -2.4% (placebo)
Proportion achieving at least 5% weight loss: 86.4% semaglutide vs 31.5% placebo
Proportion achieving at least 10% weight loss: 69%
Proportion achieving at least 15% weight loss: 50%
Proportion achieving at least 20% weight loss: 32%

CONFIRMED SECONDARY ENDPOINTS AT WEEK 68
Waist circumference change: -13.54 cm (semaglutide) vs -4.13 cm (placebo)
Estimated treatment difference: -9.42 cm (95% CI -10.30 to -8.53)
ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03548935 results: -14.1 plus or minus 9.6 cm (semaglutide)

C-reactive protein (CRP, inflammation marker):
Reduced by 44% vs placebo (estimated treatment difference -44%, 95% CI -49 to -39)
Source: Wadden et al. PMC9713290, STEP 1/2/3 CRP analysis

Systolic blood pressure, HbA1c, fasting lipids: all significantly improved vs placebo
Physical functioning (SF-36 v2): significantly improved vs placebo
Body composition (DXA subpopulation): fat mass reduced more than lean mass

CLINICAL INSIGHT FOR WEIGHT PLATEAU PATIENTS
The scale is one signal among many. During plateau periods, waist circumference,
blood pressure, and CRP continue to respond to semaglutide even when scale
weight is stable. A 9.42 cm average waist reduction over 68 weeks does not
happen in a straight line. Plateau on the scale does not mean plateau in
visceral fat or cardiovascular risk markers.

STEP 4 TRIAL - SEMAGLUTIDE WITHDRAWAL AND MAINTENANCE
Source: Rubino D et al. JAMA. 2021;325(14):1414-1425 (NCT03548987)
Design: 20-week open-label run-in (all received semaglutide 2.4mg),
then randomized 2:1 to continue semaglutide (n=535) or switch to placebo (n=268)
for 48 additional weeks. 73 sites across 10 countries.

WEIGHT OUTCOMES (week 20 to week 68)
Continued semaglutide: additional -7.9% body weight
Switched to placebo: +6.9% body weight regain
Estimated treatment difference: -14.8 percentage points (95% CI -16.0 to -13.5)
Total at week 68: -17.4% (continued semaglutide) vs -5.0% (switched to placebo)

CARDIOMETABOLIC OUTCOMES (week 20 to week 68)
Waist circumference: mean treatment difference -9.7 cm (95% CI -10.9 to -8.5)
Systolic blood pressure: remained stable in continued semaglutide group,
increased in placebo group. Mean difference: -3.9 mmHg (95% CI -5.8 to -2.0)

KEY CLINICAL INSIGHT FOR PLATEAUED PATIENTS
Patients who reached the maintenance dose and stopped semaglutide lost most
of their cardiometabolic gains. Patients who continued therapy maintained and
extended them. Staying on the drug through a scale plateau is clinically
meaningful. The cardiometabolic benefits of staying on are real and measurable
even when weight is temporarily stable.

STEP 1 EXTENSION - ONE YEAR AFTER STOPPING
Source: Rubino S et al. Diabetes Obes Metab. 2022 (PMC9542252). n=327 participants.
After stopping semaglutide at week 68:
Weight regained by week 120: mean 11.6 percentage points (SD 7.7)
Net loss from baseline at week 120: -5.6% (SD 8.9%)
Approximately two-thirds of weight lost was regained within 1 year of stopping.
Cardiometabolic improvements reverted toward baseline values.
