Hackathon walkthrough

GoaLPost¹

An SMS check-in system that notices when someone on a GLP-1 medication is about to drop off — and acts before they disappear.

Built for care teams. Explained for everyone — no coding required.

GoaLPost¹ Slide 1
The problem

Many people start GLP-1 therapy. Too many stop.

Real-world pattern

Dropout is common in the first months

Especially for weight-loss patients. Side effects, stalled weight, insurance/cost surprises, and simply going quiet are the usual reasons.

The gap

Care teams can’t text everyone every week

Manual outreach doesn’t scale. Waiting until someone complains means you’ve often already lost them.

GoaLPost¹’s job: watch a panel of patients automatically, spot who is drifting, guess why, and either send a helpful text or put a human on the case.
GoaLPost¹ Slide 2
What a patient sees

One simple weekly-style check-in

“Hi Maria, week 12 check-in from GoaLPost¹. How is your GLP-1 journey going?”

  • Reply 1 — Going well
  • Reply 2 — Having side effects
  • Reply 3 — Not seeing results

That’s the entire patient interface. No app to install. The intelligence sits on the care-team side.

GoaLPost¹ Slide 3
The big idea

Nobody has to press “Send Check-in”

One decision engine runs on a schedule. It asks three questions every time:

1

Who is due?

Higher risk → contact more often. Lower risk → leave them alone longer.

2

How worried are we?

A risk score estimates chance of stopping therapy in the next ~90 days.

3

What should we do?

Send a targeted text, escalate to a human, or simply wait until next due.

The same engine powers the live product and a months-long simulation — so the demo isn’t a fake parallel story.

GoaLPost¹ Slide 5
Step 1–2 explained

Who gets contacted, and how often?

Green

Every 14 days

Looking stable. Don’t nag.

Amber

Every 7 days

Elevated concern. Stay close.

Red

Every 3 days

High dropout risk. Tight loop.

First 4 weeks override: nobody waits two weeks early on. Titration (dose ramp-up) is when nausea and early quitters cluster, so the maximum gap is one week regardless of color.
GoaLPost¹ Slide 6
Step 3 explained — without the jargon

What is a “risk score”?

Think of it as a weather forecast for “will this person still be on therapy in ~90 days?” — a number between 0 and 1.

What goes into the forecast

  • How long they’ve been on therapy
  • Whether weight seems stuck (from their reply, for now)
  • Side-effect reports
  • Insurance type & income band
  • How many times they said “not seeing results”
  • How many check-ins they ignored

What comes out

A probability → turned into green / amber / red.

A likely barrier — the main reason the forecast got worse:

Plateau · Side effects · Cost

We use a standard prediction model plus an explanation tool (SHAP) that points at which factors mattered most for this patient. You don’t need to know the math — the dashboard shows the conclusion.

GoaLPost¹ Slide 7
Step 4 — the playbook

A written clinical policy, not a black box

Every rule lives in one readable list — a clinician can review it.

Plateau
Says “not seeing results” and risk is elevated → encouraging text grounded in published trial evidence (scale can stall while blood pressure / waist / labs still improve).
Side effects
Reply 2 → practical GI guidance. Two reports in a month → escalate to care team.
Cost
Model flags coverage/cost as the driver → navigation text + benefits-check task for a human.
Silence
One missed check-in → gentle nudge. Two missed → outreach call task.
Sustained red
Red on two scorings in a row → nurse call task.
Doing well
Reply 1 → short acknowledgment; cadence can stretch out.
GoaLPost¹ Slide 8
Trust & safety

Guardrails so the system can’t spam people

Message limits

  • Max 2 outbound texts per patient per week
  • No duplicate message body within 14 days
  • Only send between 9am and 8pm
  • At most one text per decision pass

Task limits

  • Don’t open a second task of the same type if one is already open
  • Cooldownoldown after closing — so “still red” doesn’t refill the queue forever
  • Queue is ranked by urgency, then by risk
In a 1,000-patient, 26-week stress test: zero weekly-cap violations and zero middle-of-the-night texts.
GoaLPost¹ Slide 9
The insight that matters most

Silence is a signal — not missing data

Before

Quiet patients looked “fine”

Week counters only moved when someone replied. A person who never answered could sit at “week 0” forever — invisible to risk logic.

Now

Non-response raises the alarm

Time on therapy advances from the calendar. Missed check-ins feed the risk score. Sustained silence creates outreach work and can mark someone lost to follow-up.

Clinically this matches intuition: people who stop answering often stop refilling.

GoaLPost¹ Slide 10
Care team experience

The dashboard leads with “Needs a human today”

1. Work queue

Open tasks ranked by priority and risk. Acknowledge or resolve.

2. Cohort pulse

How many red / amber / green, response rate, retention curve.

3. Patient roster

Filter by tier, silence, status. See who is due next.

Humans only appear when the policy decides their time is worth more than a text. That is the product for a clinic — not another inbox of every SMS.
GoaLPost¹ Slide 11
How we proved it

A fair A/B test over virtual months

Control arm

Prompts only

Patients still get check-in texts and still get scored. Interventions and escalations are turned off.

Intervention arm

Full playbook

Same check-ins + plateau / GI / cost messages + care-team tasks.

Both arms: 1,000 simulated patients, 26 weeks, day-by-day, calling the same decision engine as production.

Honest caveat: how much a text “helps” is an assumption we declare up front. The simulation proves the machinery works at scale — right people, right day, guardrails hold, queue drains. It does not prove clinical effect sizes. Those need a real pilot.

Live demo path

What to click when you show this

  1. Open the dashboard — start with the work queue, not the patient table.
  2. Acknowledge / resolve a task — show the human loop.
  3. Run scheduler tick — prove it only contacts people who are due (second click does nothing).
  4. Simulate reply “3” — watch risk, barrier, and the plateau message appear.
  5. Optional: reply “2” — different branch, side-effect guidance.
  6. Show simulation chart — control vs intervention, then state the caveat.

What we built

  • A risk forecast + reason (plateau / side effects / cost / silence)

Hackathon Round 2

Where this goes next

Highest leverage

Make it feel real

  • Live SMS (Twilio) on one phone number
  • Tune green/amber/red with a clinician
  • Replace assumed “text helps by X%” with literature or advisor estimates
  • Hook real weight / labs when available (today’s reply is a stand-in)
Then

Path to a pilot

  • Dashboard login + audit trail of who resolved what
  • Pre-register a small clinic endpoint: 90-day persistence
  • Keep the engine; don’t chase fancier models on fake labels
Round 1 proved the system. Round 2 should prove contact with reality — real messages, clinical cadence, pilot design.
